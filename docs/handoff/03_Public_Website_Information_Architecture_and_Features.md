## Website mission
The website should convert a spiritually motivated but uncertain visitor into an informed, qualified conversation and eventually a well-prepared traveller. It must do more than inspire. It must answer the questions that determine trust: current route state, operator identity, service reality, group fit, comfort, documents, payment conditions and what happens when the mountains disrupt the plan.
The website is the authoritative, structured source of truth. Social media attracts attention; WhatsApp creates conversation; the website establishes credibility and preserves the current version of journey information, policies and status.
| Primary website outcome: A visitor understands the journey, believes the team is truthful and local, and takes the correct next action without being pressured or misled. |

## Primary user goals
A visitor should be able to:
- Understand what Adi Kailash and Om Parvat journey involves
- See whether current or upcoming departures are open, conditional, waitlisted or unavailable
- Compare service tiers through concrete differences
- Understand route, altitude, accommodation, group size, inclusions and exclusions
- Know whether the trip appears suitable enough to discuss further
- Learn the expected document and preparation process
- See the disclosed operator and local team
- Start a WhatsApp conversation, request a call or submit an enquiry
- Reserve or join a protected waitlist when the approved business policy allows it
- Find truthful answers without downloading a brochure as the only source

## Business goals
- Build brand authority before and beyond paid acquisition
- Capture high-intent search and answer-engine demand
- Qualify leads before human calls
- Reduce repetitive questions without removing human reassurance
- Present packages and departures consistently
- Generate consented WhatsApp and callback leads with attribution
- Create a durable content and route-intelligence asset
- Support both direct operation and a disclosed operating partner
- Provide a foundation for international and private enquiries

## Homepage specification
The homepage should move through a deliberate emotional and practical sequence.

### 1. Hero: the calling and the promise
Purpose: Create emotional resonance and immediately explain what the company does.
Content:
- Working brand and concise descriptor
- Hero line such as "Some journeys begin with a plan. Others begin with a calling."
- One practical support sentence
- Primary CTA: Explore Adi Kailash Journey
- Secondary CTA: Speak to a Journey Guide
- Current status cue: season, waitlist or last route update
- Original or clearly illustrative hero image
Do not include: A carousel, multiple offers, fake scarcity, long autoplay video or a price without context.

### 2. Current journey status
Purpose: Prove that the site understands the present operating reality.
Content:
- Status label
- Last verified time
- Short impact statement
- Link to live-status detail
- WhatsApp notification subscription option

### 3. Flagship journey overview
Purpose: Translate inspiration into a concrete product.
Content:
- Duration, gateway, service tiers, broad difficulty and altitude context
- Key sacred locations
- Immediate journey CTA
- Clear note that exact departure, permit and route conditions are dynamic

### 4. Why travellers choose the team
Use evidence-led pillars:
- Local roots in Pithoragarh and Kumaon
- Verified stays and transport
- Transparent comfort and route information
- Named support and small controlled departures
- Preparation and disruption care

### 5. Journey families
Show a restrained set of cards:
- Adi Kailash and Om Parvat
- Kumaon spiritual circuit
- Paths of Mahadev
- Private sacred journeys
Do not display unlaunched journeys as purchasable. Use "planned" or "register interest" states.

### 6. The journey as a passage
A visual or textual timeline:
- The call
- The conversation
- The preparation
- The pilgrimage
- The return
This is a strong place for an optional route animation or later 3D link.

### 7. Real accommodation and route truth
Show a small, honest comparison of lower-route comfort and upper-route reality. Link to the detailed page. This section should reduce future disappointment rather than merely decorate the homepage.

### 8. Traveller stories
Use verified, permissioned testimonials with journey, month and traveller context. Avoid anonymous five-star quotes without evidence. Early versions may use founder/local stories rather than invented reviews.

### 9. Local story and team
Introduce the founders' Pithoragarh roots, local operations and why the company exists. Show real faces and roles.

### 10. Preparation lead magnet or consultation
Offer one meaningful action:
- Download or receive an Adi Kailash readiness guide
- Get notified when permits or departures open
- Book a planning call
The form should collect consent and lead context rather than only email.

### 11. Final CTA
Return to the central emotional promise and offer WhatsApp, callback or journey exploration.

## Journey-detail page specification
The journey page is the most important conversion asset. It should be a complete, crawlable and shareable source of truth.

### Required modules

#### Journey header
- Journey name and concise essence
- Status, availability or enquiry state
- Duration and gateway
- Primary CTA
- Service tier indication
- Last content review date where relevant

#### Why this journey matters
Provide spiritual, cultural and landscape context with clear distinctions between tradition, local belief and verified practical information. Avoid turning mythology into a hard operational claim.

#### At-a-glance summary
| Field | Example content type |
| Duration | Number of nights and days |
| Start and end | Gateway city or pickup boundary |
| Highest altitude | Approved factual value or range |
| Travel style | Shared, comfort or private |
| Group size | Maximum and typical |
| Accommodation | Clear summary by route segment |
| Physical demand | Plain-language indicator and explanation |
| Permit | Required and subject to current process |
| Best period | Content-managed season guidance |
| Current state | Available, pending, waitlist or unavailable |

#### Who the journey is for
- Ideal traveller profile
- When a private or slower itinerary may be better
- When a customer should seek medical advice before continuing
- What the journey is not

#### Detailed itinerary
Each day or stage should include:
- Origin and destination
- Mode and broad travel time
- Altitude transition where useful
- Major spiritual or cultural moments
- Accommodation type
- Meals and inclusions if approved
- Practical notes
- Dynamic caveat for route-dependent plans
The itinerary must be structured content rather than a single image or PDF.

#### Route and optional interactive experience
P0: Clear 2D map or route sequence.
P1/P2: Optional interactive or 3D terrain view with stops, altitude, road/walk segments and accommodation. The text itinerary remains the accessible source of truth.

#### Accommodation reality
- Actual categories by location
- Original or verified images
- Occupancy and room-sharing expectations
- Bathroom, heating, electricity, network and luggage notes when approved
- Difference between tiers
- Substitution rule and notification policy

#### Transport and group model
- Vehicle type range, not a guaranteed registration before confirmation
- Seating and luggage expectations
- Shared versus private distinctions
- Pickup boundary
- Driver and vehicle details timing
- Local vehicle changes if route requires them

#### Inclusions and exclusions
Use explicit, grouped lists. Exclusions should be as visible as inclusions. Avoid vague "all sightseeing" language.

#### Service-tier comparison
Compare tangible elements such as group size, pace, lower-route stay quality, pickup scope, coordinator level, luggage support, room policy and customisation.

#### Departure list
Show dated departures as separate records with states:
- Open
- Limited
- Conditional or permit pending
- Waitlist
- Sold out
- Suspended
- Rescheduled
- Completed
Each departure may have a different operator, price, capacity, payment condition and status note.

#### Documents and readiness
Show a high-level checklist and link to the detailed guide. Do not imply that completing the website checklist guarantees permit approval.

#### Health, altitude and safety
Use reviewed educational content. Encourage qualified medical consultation and explain that suitability is individual. Do not automatically exclude or approve based only on age.

#### Disruption and protection
Explain what happens if permits pause, roads close, a departure is rescheduled or a traveller cannot proceed. Use approved policy language and versioning.

#### Frequently asked questions
Questions should reflect real sales conversations, not generic SEO filler. Include direct answers and links to detailed pages.

#### Final enquiry block
Pre-fill the journey and departure context into WhatsApp or the enquiry form. Offer a planning call for complex groups.

## Departure page and state model
A departure is not merely a date. It is an operational object with capacity, operator, vendors, status and traveller conditions.

### Public fields
- Date range
- Journey and tier
- Starting gateway
- Current state
- Seats or availability language approved by operations
- Price or enquiry-only state
- Operating entity or partner disclosure
- Deposit condition
- Last status update
- Primary action

### State behaviour
| State | Public behaviour |
| Open | Allow enquiry and approved reservation action |
| Limited | Show limited capacity only when inventory is trusted |
| Conditional | Explain the dependency and protect the payment expectation |
| Permit pending | Emphasise waitlist or protected reservation rules |
| Waitlist | Collect priority and flexibility without claiming a seat |
| Sold out | Offer alternate date or private enquiry |
| Suspended | Stop payment; show reason and next update process |
| Rescheduled | Present original and proposed date with affected-traveller support |
| Completed | Preserve as an archive or traveller-story reference where appropriate |

## Live route and permit status page
This page should become a signature trust and organic-discovery asset.

### Required content
- Overall status: open, limited, suspended, closed, unverified or not in season
- Permit status
- Key route-segment status
- Impact on upcoming departures
- Last verified date and time
- Source type: official notice, operating partner, field coordinator, vendor or other approved source
- What is confirmed and what is not
- Next expected update
- Update history
- Subscription via WhatsApp or email
- Disclaimer that conditions may change rapidly

### Governance
Only authorised roles may publish a verified status. A status should expire or become visibly stale after a defined interval. The system should preserve who changed it, when and why.

### SEO and AEO design
The status summary must exist in readable page text, not only a widget. Use direct answers, timestamps, headings and structured update history. Do not create a false "live" claim when the update is manually verified.

## Private and international enquiry flow
International and complex private groups should not be forced through a standard package checkout.
The page should collect:
- Country or current location
- Preferred date range and flexibility
- Group size and age range
- Experience and comfort preference
- Gateway and airport needs
- Spiritual, cultural or trekking interests
- Language
- Time-zone and consultation preference
- Any accessibility or support needs the traveller chooses to disclose
The outcome is a consultation and tailored proposal, not an instant guarantee.

## Contact and enquiry experience
Provide three clear methods:
1. WhatsApp conversation
2. Schedule or request a call
3. Structured enquiry form
Display realistic support hours and expected response time. For urgent active-trip support, use a separate authenticated or booking-aware contact path rather than the public sales number alone.

## CMS and content model overview
The site should be driven by structured content for normal business updates.

### Core content types
- Brand configuration
- Journey
- Service tier
- Departure
- Destination
- Itinerary day or stage
- Accommodation or stay
- Route segment
- Status update
- FAQ
- Guide article
- Team member
- Testimonial or traveller story
- Policy and policy version
- Campaign or landing page
- Media asset with provenance
- Operator and legal disclosure

### Content relationships
- A journey has many tiers and departures
- A departure belongs to a journey and tier
- A journey references destinations, itinerary stages and route segments
- A departure references an operator and selected vendors
- A status update may affect journeys, departures and route segments
- A policy version applies to reservations or departures
- Media assets may represent actual or illustrative content and must be labelled internally

## Conversion paths

### High-intent path
Search or referral -> journey page -> departure -> WhatsApp with context -> qualification -> human call -> proposal -> reservation.

### Uncertain route path
Social or search -> live status -> notification opt-in -> readiness guide -> nurture -> departure open -> WhatsApp or reservation.

### Premium path
International or private landing page -> structured enquiry -> consultation -> custom proposal -> booking.

### Content path
Guide or journal -> relevant journey module -> status or preparation page -> WhatsApp or email nurture.

## Forms and data collection principles
- Ask only what is necessary for the current stage
- Collect explicit communication consent
- Preserve source, page and journey context
- Avoid asking for sensitive documents in a public enquiry form
- Do not require account creation to ask a question
- Explain expected response time
- Provide validation that is helpful rather than punitive
- Allow a group organiser to continue without entering every companion immediately
- Protect against spam without creating inaccessible challenges

## Search, performance and accessibility requirements

### Search and answer-engine readiness
- Essential content must be crawlable HTML
- Use descriptive titles and headings
- Use direct answer summaries where appropriate
- Maintain original local content and real author ownership
- Add structured metadata that matches visible content
- Avoid mass-generated location pages without unique value
- Make live status, price context, documents and policies easy to cite

### Performance
- Prioritise fast mobile first render
- Optimise images and use responsive formats
- Lazy-load nonessential video, 3D and maps
- Keep enquiry and status usable when rich media fails
- Avoid third-party scripts that materially slow the page without measured value

### Accessibility
- Keyboard and screen-reader usable navigation
- Sufficient contrast and scalable typography
- Descriptive form labels and errors
- Status not communicated by colour alone
- Alternative text for meaningful imagery
- Reduced-motion support
- Accessible text fallback for maps and 3D
- Readable Hindi and English layouts

## Launch acceptance scenarios
The public website is ready when the team can demonstrate all of the following:
1. A mobile visitor can understand the flagship journey and reach the correct enquiry path without opening a PDF.
2. A visitor sees a departure's true state and cannot pay for a suspended or unapproved state.
3. A WhatsApp enquiry contains the page or journey context needed by sales.
4. The operations owner can update a route-status summary and timestamp without a code deployment.
5. A brand name or logo change can be made centrally without editing every page.
6. A visitor can compare tiers through concrete service differences.
7. International and private visitors can request a consultation without being forced into a standard shared departure.
8. Essential content remains readable on a slow mobile connection when maps and video do not load.
9. Legal operator, policies, contact and consent information are easy to locate.
10. Analytics distinguishes content interest, lead creation, qualified lead, reservation and confirmed payment.
