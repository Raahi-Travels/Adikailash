## Management purpose
This document turns the product vision into an executable programme without prescribing final implementation details. It is designed for a three-brother founding team working with coding agents, designers, content contributors, operators and vendors.
The greatest launch risk is not missing an advanced feature. It is building disconnected surfaces while core decisions about operator responsibility, status truth, deposits, refunds, content ownership and customer communication remain unresolved.
| Programme north star: Launch the smallest coherent operating system that can acquire a traveller, make a truthful promise, prepare the group, run a controlled departure and close the trip with accountability. |

## Delivery principles
1. Operations and product launch together. Do not launch payment or confirmed dates before the operating process exists.
2. Manual is acceptable; invisible is not. A founder may perform a task manually in the first version, but ownership, status and evidence should still be visible.
3. Policies precede automation. Do not automate refunds, medical guidance, status publishing or vendor substitution before the founders approve the rule.
4. One source of truth. A decision should not require editing the website, bot, brochure and five spreadsheets independently.
5. Controlled batches before scale. The team should validate service quality with a small number of departures before increasing ad spend and capacity.
6. Feature delivery includes operating adoption. A dashboard that operations does not update is not complete.
7. Brand is provisional. Build and document for replacement.
8. Trust defects are severity-one defects. False availability, incorrect payment status, exposed documents and misleading operator claims block release.

## Recommended delivery phases

### Phase 0: Foundational decisions and operating readiness
Goal: Resolve the business rules that product and content cannot safely invent.

#### Required decisions
- Working brand and replacement process
- Legal entity and public legal identity
- Own tour registration versus disclosed operating partner for each departure
- Who contracts, invoices and receives payment
- Initial journey, itinerary and service tiers
- Deposit, protected reservation and confirmation rules
- Cancellation, rescheduling, route disruption and refund policy
- Traveller document process
- Route-status source and authorised publisher
- Support hours, escalation and emergency ownership
- Initial supplier shortlist and backup approach
- Languages at launch

#### Outputs
- Decision register
- Approved journey facts and itinerary
- Provisional brand tokens
- Policy drafts reviewed by professional advisers where required
- Operator and supplier responsibility map
- Content and media inventory
- Initial measurement plan
Exit condition: The team can explain exactly what a customer is buying, from whom, under what conditions and what happens if the departure cannot proceed.

### Phase 1: Conversion and lead-management launch
Goal: Start acquiring and qualifying demand without overpromising.

#### Scope
- Mobile-first homepage
- Flagship Adi Kailash and Om Parvat journey page
- Departure or waitlist states
- Live route and permit status
- Documents, accommodation and preparation basics
- WhatsApp and callback entry points with context
- CRM stages, ownership and next action
- Basic analytics and attribution
- Brand and legal configuration
- Policy and operator disclosure pages

#### Manual allowances
- Human proposal preparation
- Manual callback scheduling
- Manual route verification
- Manual content publishing with approval
- Manual reservation review before payment link
Exit condition: A paid or organic visitor can become a qualified lead and receive a truthful proposal without the team losing context.

### Phase 2: Reservation and traveller preparation
Goal: Turn qualified demand into controlled reservations and ready travellers.

#### Scope
- Proposal and reservation records
- Payment and receipt integration
- Booking state and policy acceptance
- Group lead and companion records
- Traveller information and document checklist
- Payment and readiness summary
- Named coordinator
- Booking-specific updates
- Downloadable itinerary or trip pack

#### Manual allowances
- Staff review of every payment and confirmation
- Staff document review
- Manual operator submission
- Manual finance reconciliation with recorded state
Exit condition: Every reserved group has a visible state, payment trail, accepted terms, preparation owner and next action.

### Phase 3: Departure operations and trip support
Goal: Run the first controlled departures through a shared operational system.

#### Scope
- Departure lifecycle and control centre
- Manifest and traveller readiness
- Accommodation, rooming, vehicles, drivers and guides
- Operating partner assignment
- Readiness gates, tasks and blockers
- Route change and customer communication
- Incident record
- Supplier payable and reconciliation summary
- Active-trip support path

#### Manual allowances
- Human supplier confirmation
- Human readiness approval
- Human disruption decisions
- Printed or offline contingency pack
Exit condition: Operations can identify and resolve every critical blocker before departure and preserve a record of what customers were told.

### Phase 4: Growth, international and service maturity
Goal: Improve conversion, authority and premium service after operational proof.

#### Scope
- Content hub and SEO/AEO clusters
- Route-status subscriptions and nurture
- Private and international consultation
- Review and referral flow
- Family sharing
- Richer trip companion
- Vendor performance and post-trip learning
- B2B ground-handling enquiry
- Better attribution to contribution
Exit condition: Acquisition can grow without creating service breakdown or losing unit-economics visibility.

### Phase 5: Differentiation and platform expansion
Goal: Add defensible experience and network capabilities.

#### Candidates
- Interactive 3D terrain and altitude experience
- Offline-first trip companion
- AI itinerary and concierge grounded in approved data
- Advanced route intelligence
- Vendor reliability scoring
- B2B partner dashboard
- Shared capabilities with the hill-mobility platform
- Multi-brand and multi-region support
Entry condition: Core journeys are profitable or strategically validated, operations reliably uses the system and content quality is strong.

## Three-founder governance model
Equal ownership should not mean every small decision requires three people. Assign accountable roles even if the final names change.

### Role archetypes

#### Founder A: Product, technology and data
- Product coherence
- Technical architecture
- Coding-agent coordination
- Data and analytics
- Security and reliability
- Brand configurability and future mobility integration

#### Founder B: Operations and supply
- Journey feasibility
- Operating partner
- Hotels, vehicles, guides and local coordinators
- Route and permit verification
- Traveller preparation and incident response
- Service-quality truth

#### Founder C: Commercial, finance and growth
- Pricing and contribution
- Payments, invoicing and refunds
- Paid acquisition and sales performance
- Partnerships and international commercial enquiries
- Customer policies with professional advisers
Actual responsibilities may be reassigned, but each domain needs one accountable owner.

## Decision rights

### Routine decisions
The accountable owner may decide within an approved budget and policy.
Examples:
- Content scheduling
- Non-material copy edits
- Lead assignment
- Routine supplier follow-up
- Minor interface improvement
- Approved campaign optimisation

### Two-of-three founder approval
Examples:
- New journey or service tier
- Material supplier replacement
- Significant campaign budget increase
- Major feature-scope change
- Hiring or long-term vendor commitment within an agreed threshold
- Customer compensation above routine policy

### Unanimous approval
Examples:
- Final brand and trademark filing
- Legal entity or ownership change
- Taking debt or signing personal guarantees
- Issuing equity
- Material grant commitment or use-of-funds change
- Changing core refund policy
- Launching a high-risk activity
- Selling or licensing core IP
- Related-party transaction involving a founder
- Major privacy or data-sharing change
- Dissolution or sale

## Product-management process

### Backlog structure
Organise work by outcomes and journeys, not only screens.
Recommended hierarchy:
- Product objective
- Workstream or journey stage
- Epic
- User outcome
- Feature or workflow
- Acceptance scenario
- Tasks proposed by implementation agents

### Priority test
A feature moves earlier when it:
- Prevents a false promise or operational failure
- Enables qualified acquisition or payment
- Reduces traveller uncertainty
- Prevents document or supplier chaos
- Protects privacy, money or safety
- Produces data needed for a major decision
A feature moves later when it:
- Is primarily decorative
- Requires content or operations that do not yet exist
- Automates an undefined policy
- Serves an unvalidated segment
- Duplicates a reliable manual process without measurable benefit

## Coding-agent engagement rules

### Assignment brief
Each agent assignment should include:
- Business outcome
- User and role
- Current manual process
- In-scope and out-of-scope states
- Source of truth
- Dependencies and open decisions
- Required analytics
- Security and privacy level
- Acceptance scenarios
- Handoff artefacts

### Agent obligations
- Ask for decisions when policy is missing
- Propose implementation options and trade-offs
- Keep brand and provider choices replaceable
- Preserve audit and manual override
- Include mobile, accessibility and failure states
- Avoid hard-coded current route, price or policy content
- Do not invent testimonial, operator, permit or safety data
- Update architecture and decision records when scope changes

### Review checkpoints
- Flow and state review before implementation
- Content and policy review before public exposure
- Security review for documents, payments and staff permissions
- Operations UAT before enabling traveller use
- Analytics verification before paid-media scaling

## Definition of Ready
A feature is ready for implementation when:
- User and business outcome are clear
- Responsible owner is named
- Required policy and content are available or explicitly mocked
- Data source and ownership are known
- States and exceptions are identified
- Privacy level is classified
- Dependencies are acknowledged
- Acceptance scenarios are written
- Manual fallback exists for launch-critical flow

## Definition of Done
A feature is done when:
- Agreed user states work on mobile and desktop as relevant
- Error, empty, pending and disrupted states are handled
- Permissions and audit expectations are met
- Content is approved and editable by the correct role
- Analytics events are verified
- Accessibility basics are checked
- Operations or sales has completed UAT
- Support and fallback are documented
- Known limitations and follow-up items are recorded
- The release does not create contradictory public or internal truth

## Quality and test strategy

### Core test categories
- Functional states
- Role and permission
- Mobile and low bandwidth
- Accessibility
- Content and translation
- Payment and reconciliation
- Messaging delivery and failure
- Data privacy and document access
- Status freshness and expiry
- Route/disruption scenarios
- Analytics and attribution
- Backup and operational fallback

### High-risk end-to-end scenarios
1. A lead starts from a route-status page, qualifies and joins a waitlist.
2. A conditional reservation is paid but the route remains suspended.
3. A route reopens and the correct waitlist audience is invited.
4. A payment succeeds but the client page times out.
5. A companion uploads the wrong document and needs correction.
6. An operating partner changes for a departure before confirmation.
7. A named hotel becomes unavailable and is substituted.
8. A confirmed departure is rescheduled.
9. A traveller raises a health concern in WhatsApp.
10. A during-trip incident requires restricted coordination.
11. The WhatsApp provider is unavailable during a critical update.
12. The brand name changes before public launch.

## Trust and safety framework

### Public-claim rules
Every material claim should be one of:
- Approved stable brand or service statement
- Content-managed journey information with review date
- Verified current status with timestamp and source type
- Customer-specific commitment attached to a proposal or booking
- Clearly labelled illustrative or aspirational content
Do not publish:
- Guaranteed route, weather, visibility, darshan or spiritual result
- Unverified traveller counts, awards or ratings
- Accommodation images that are not the actual or clearly representative property
- Medical suitability claims
- Operator registration presented under the wrong entity
- "Live" status without a functioning verification process

### Health and altitude
The company should:
- Provide reviewed educational guidance
- Encourage qualified medical consultation
- Explain required medical documents where applicable
- Record acknowledgement and operational support needs
- Maintain emergency contacts and response procedures
The company should not diagnose, prescribe, certify fitness or imply risk-free travel.

### Traveller conduct and sacred respect
A responsible-travel code may address:
- Respect for local communities, temples and rituals
- Photography consent
- Waste and environmental care
- Behaviour toward drivers, hosts and staff
- Alcohol, substances and prohibited conduct where relevant
- Group punctuality and safety instructions
- Route and authority restrictions

## Compliance workstream
This is a management checklist, not legal advice. The founders should obtain appropriate professional review for the final operating model.

### Entity and operator
- Final legal entity and constitutional objects
- Tourism or tour-operator registration requirements
- Operating-partner agreement if used
- Adventure or trekking partner requirements for applicable activities
- Vendor contracts and responsibility allocation
- Customer contract and invoice identity
- Insurance requirements

### Customer terms
- Package description and inclusions
- Price and tax treatment
- Deposit or reservation condition
- Cancellation and refund
- Rescheduling and transfer
- Force majeure and route disruption
- Minimum group or permit dependency
- Accommodation and vehicle substitution
- Traveller responsibility and conduct
- Complaint and dispute process
- Operating partner disclosure

### Data and communications
- Privacy notice
- Communication consent
- Essential versus promotional messaging
- Identity-document collection and retention
- Access controls and staff confidentiality
- International data considerations as the business expands
- Cookie or tracking consent as applicable
- Image and testimonial permission

### Payments and finance
- Business bank and payment recipient
- Invoice and receipt process
- Refund authority
- Reconciliation
- Customer deposit handling
- Grant accounting separation if relevant
- Tax and accounting review

### Content and intellectual property
- Brand and trademark clearance
- Domain and social-handle ownership
- Code and design IP assignment
- Image and music licensing
- Traveller-content permissions
- AI-generated asset disclosure where relevant
- Supplier image rights

## Policy-versioning requirements
Policies will change. The product should preserve:
- Version identifier
- Effective date
- Public content
- Approver
- Which proposal, reservation or booking accepted it
- Material-change notice where required
Do not silently replace the policy attached to an existing booking.

## Incident and crisis governance

### Severity model
| Severity | Example | Response expectation |
| Critical | Serious safety incident, missing traveller, major data exposure, payment system error affecting many customers | Immediate founder and specialist escalation |
| High | Departure-wide route cancellation, major supplier failure, incorrect public status or exposed document | Rapid cross-functional response and customer communication |
| Medium | Hotel substitution, repeated transport delay, individual payment dispute | Named owner and time-bound resolution |
| Low | Content error, minor support delay, non-critical usability issue | Normal triage and correction |

### Crisis communication principles
- Confirm facts before speculation
- Acknowledge quickly
- State what is known and unknown
- Assign a spokesperson or owner
- Give next update time
- Avoid religious reassurance as a substitute for operational action
- Preserve records
- Conduct a post-incident review

## Launch readiness checklist

### Business and legal
- Final or approved working brand displayed correctly
- Legal entity and operating partner confirmed
- Customer contract, invoice and payment recipient aligned
- Cancellation, refund and disruption policy approved
- Privacy and communication consent published
- Required registrations or partner arrangements confirmed

### Product and content
- Home, flagship journey, departure, live status and preparation pages complete
- Real operational images verified
- Prices, dates, status and inclusions reviewed
- Brand configuration centralised
- Hindi and English content reviewed where launched
- No placeholder testimonials or unsupported claims

### Sales and support
- WhatsApp and callback tested
- Lead ownership and response rota active
- Proposal and reservation process documented
- Escalation and complaint ownership clear
- International enquiry path tested if advertised

### Booking and finance
- Payment success, failure, duplicate and refund scenarios tested
- Policy acceptance recorded
- Booking state matches payment and departure state
- Finance reconciliation process active
- Refund authority and timeline defined

### Operations
- Departure owner and operating partner assigned
- Traveller and document workflows tested
- Supplier confirmations and backups recorded
- Route-status owner and update process active
- Readiness checklist and final trip pack tested
- Incident and emergency contacts prepared

### Technology and security
- Staff roles and access reviewed
- Sensitive documents secured
- Backups and recovery checked
- Monitoring and alert ownership set
- Analytics verified
- Public site performance and accessibility checked
- Export or manual fallback available for critical operations

### Growth
- Campaign pages match operational truth
- Conversion events tested through CRM and payment
- Daily lead capacity defined
- Ad budgets capped for validation
- Status subscribers and marketing consent separated
- Review and referral claims disabled until real evidence exists

## First-departure retrospective
Within a short period after each early departure, review:
- What customers expected versus received
- Which information reduced or created confusion
- Sales objections and promise mismatches
- Document and permit delays
- Supplier reliability
- Route-status accuracy
- Communication timing
- Portal and admin usage
- Manual work that should remain manual
- Manual work worth productising
- Incidents and near misses
- Unit economics and refund exposure
- Traveller stories and permissions
Prioritise the next release from actual operational evidence, not only founder imagination.

## Open founder decisions
Before the coding team treats the product as production-ready, the founders should explicitly approve:
- Final brand or date by which the working brand will be reconsidered
- Consumer brand versus legal entity naming
- First operating partner or own-registration path
- Exact launch journey and service tiers
- Reservation and confirmation states
- Payment schedule
- Cancellation, refund and route-disruption rules
- Support hours and escalation
- Content approval owner
- Route-status verification process
- Initial target departure capacity
- International service boundary
- Relationship between this product and the mobility startup

## Final release principle
A launch should be delayed for a false promise, insecure document flow, incorrect payment state or unclear operator responsibility. It should not be delayed because the optional 3D map, AI concierge or advanced dashboard is not yet perfect.
The first release wins when the team can make one honest promise, preserve it across every channel and deliver it through a well-run journey.
